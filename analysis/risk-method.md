# Privacy Risk Method

## Scoring model

Each scenario receives a likelihood score and an impact score from 1 to 5. The product gives a 1–25 inherent risk score.

| Score | Likelihood | Interpretation |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Rare | Requires exceptional conditions or several failures |
| 2 | Unlikely | Plausible but infrequent in the modeled workflow |
| 3 | Possible | Credible during normal operation or a common failure |
| 4 | Likely | Expected repeatedly without a specific control |
| 5 | Almost certain | Inherent in the current design or continuously exposed |

| Score | Impact | Interpretation for data subjects |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1 | Negligible | Minimal and easily reversible inconvenience |
| 2 | Minor | Limited loss of privacy with short recovery |
| 3 | Moderate | Meaningful loss of control, confidentiality, or trust |
| 4 | Major | Serious academic, social, financial, or autonomy effect |
| 5 | Severe | Broad or lasting harm involving sensitive or complete records |

## Priority bands

| Product | Priority | Expected response |
| ---: | --- | --- |
| 1–4 | Low | Accept with owner review or improve during normal maintenance |
| 5–9 | Medium | Plan and track a proportionate control |
| 10–16 | High | Assign an owner, deadline, and verification evidence |
| 17–25 | Critical | Redesign or reduce exposure before approval |

## Rules

- Scores describe the supplied design scenario, not a measured production environment.
- Privacy impact concerns the person, not only the institution.
- Existing controls receive no credit without evidence.
- Residual risk requires rescoring after implementation and testing.
- A low numerical score does not override a legal obligation.
